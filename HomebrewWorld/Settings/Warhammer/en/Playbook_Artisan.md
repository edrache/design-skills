## Background

Pick one.

---

**Guild Journeyman**

You completed your apprenticeship, passed the examinations, paid the dues. You are part of the organisation — you have its support, its network, and its seal on your licence. You also have obligations, fees, and rules you cannot break without consequences. When you present your work or services bearing a guild seal, you always find a buyer at a fair price. The GM will say who wants to buy and what they expect.

When you introduce yourself to the other PCs, ask one or more of the following:
- Which of you knows my name in the city and can vouch for me?
- Which of you knows why I left the workshop and took to the road?
- Which of you used my services and didn't pay — or paid far too well?
- Which of you understands that my guild can help me, but can also call me back?

---

**Free Craftsman**

You belong to no guild — either you were expelled or you never let yourself be roped in. Your freedom has a price: no protection, no licence, no network. But also no obligations. When you offer your services without asking about papers, you always find someone who prefers not to ask — a smuggler, a fugitive, someone with a secret. Roll +CHA. On a 10+ you agree terms and do the job without complications. On a 7-9 you get the work, but someone finds out you're operating without a licence.

When you introduce yourself to the other PCs, ask one or more of the following:
- Which of you knows what happened to my master or my guild?
- Which of you covers for my lack of documents — and what do they want for it?
- Which of you understands I work outside the law because the law failed me?
- Which of you hired me first when no one else would take the risk?

---

**Military Craftsman**

You worked with an army or a navy — repairing weapons in camp, patching boats in harbour, shoeing horses on a battlefield. You saw war from the rear: the dirt, the urgency, the blood, and the question "will it hold?" When you repair or create equipment under field conditions — no workshop, under time pressure or threat — you always manage. The GM will say what you're missing and what price haste will cost.

When you introduce yourself to the other PCs, ask one or more of the following:
- Which of you served in the same campaign or garrison?
- Which of you is wearing something I repaired in the field?
- Which of you knows what I did to get out of military service?
- Which of you understands the difference between "good enough" and "won't hold"?

## Starting Moves

**Trade in the Hands**
When you create or repair an object within your Craft, using appropriate tools and materials, the GM tells you what it takes and how long. The result is always solid and lasting. When you work in haste or with missing materials, roll +CON. On a 10+ the work is good despite the conditions. On a 7-9 it holds, but with a compromise: shorter lifespan, a hidden weakness, or a material you meant to save.

**Assess the Work**
When you hold a crafted object — weapon, armour, tool, fabric, structure — you always know whether it is good or poor, and why. The GM tells you: what it is made of, how it was made, and what it is worth. For further questions, roll +INT. On a 10+ ask three, on a 7-9 ask one:
- Who made this and where?
- Is there a hidden flaw or modification?
- What is this really for?
- How much could you get for it and from whom?

**Improvised Tools**
When you need a tool you don't have and you have access to materials — wood, metal, rope, anything — you can make one on the spot. An improvised tool works once without trouble. For continued use, roll +DEX. On a 7-9 it holds, but barely.

**Working Strength**
Years at the bench gave you strength and endurance out of proportion to your size. When you perform physical work requiring force or stamina — lifting, breaking shackles, forcing a way through — roll +CON. On a 10+ you do it without effort or cost. On a 7-9 you succeed, but you are exhausted or something breaks in the process.

**Technical Eye**
You understand how things are made. When you try to disassemble something, force a mechanism, or find a weak point in a construction, you always know where to strike. The GM tells you the weakness of the thing in question — lock, gate, armour, machine — before you even ask.

## Advances

- **Masterwork:** Once between rests you can create or repair something exceptional. The result has one special quality (sharper, lighter, concealed ornamentation, better protection) — you and the GM work out the details.
- **Guild Apprentice:** A young trainee joins you, following simple orders, carrying materials, and learning the trade. Treat them as a hireling from the Core Rules. They can independently use the Assess the Work move at the 7-9 level.
- **Black Gold:** When you price something for sale or negotiate payment for a service, you always get more than market value. The GM will say what and from whom — someone always needs it more than they realise.
- **Material Adaptation:** You can work with materials normally outside your Craft. A blacksmith can stitch leather into armour; a carpenter can lay a stone wall. Roll +INT instead of the usual stat — on a 10+ the result is fully functional.
- **Built to Last:** The things you make endure. When an object of your making would be destroyed or damaged through normal use or in combat, you may ask the GM: "Does this really break now?" The GM must justify the answer.
- **Improved Stat:** Increase one of your stats by +1, to a maximum of +2.
- **Greater Stat** *(Requires: Improved Stat and at least one other Advance)*: Increase one of your stats by +1, to a maximum of +3.

## Drives

- Create a masterwork that will outlast you and be carried by generations.
- Prove to the guild — or to those who cast you out of it — that your craft is the finest in the region.
- Establish a workshop, tools, and a reputation worthy of a guild master.
- Find the material, technique, or trade secret that will transform what you are capable of making.
