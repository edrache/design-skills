*You have a strong stomach and steady hands. You practise the art of medicine, doing your best to save lives — though the Empire is convinced that medicine is mostly a matter of paying the Guild the right amount.*

## Background

Pick one.

---

**Certified Surgeon**

You completed the Academy and paid the Guild a fair sum for your licence. Your knowledge is systematic — and expensively priced. When you diagnose a disease, wound, or poisoning, the GM tells you: what it is, what threatens the patient, and the most effective treatment — plus one complication that might surprise even you.

When you introduce yourself to the other PCs, ask one or more of the following:
- Which of you dismissed my qualifications until you needed help yourself?
- Which of you owes me payment for something we'd both rather forget?
- Which of you gives me clinical unease — something about them doesn't sit right?
- Which of you knows someone in the Guild who is currently making my life difficult?

---

**Battlefield Barber-Surgeon**

You have no formal training — just scars and years of learning from the wounded. Cheaper than the Guild, faster in the field, with no illusions about what medicine actually looks like. When you treat someone in combat or under pressure, roll +INT. On a 10+ choose two: you restore 1d8+1 HP, you use a minimum of supplies, the patient feels no pain during the procedure. On a 7-9 choose one.

When you introduce yourself to the other PCs, ask one or more of the following:
- Which of you knows me from some unfortunate episode before I started healing people?
- Which of you doesn't fully trust me — and has some basis for that?
- Which of you witnessed me doing something that should get me struck off?
- Which of you knows where I really got my instruments?

---

**Anatomy Scholar**

You are obsessively interested in how the body works — living and dead. The Cult of Morr forbids the study of corpses. The law is ambiguous on the matter. You are not. When you examine a body — living, dead, human, or otherwise — you may always ask the GM one question and receive an honest answer: how it died, what weakens it, what it needs to survive.

When you introduce yourself to the other PCs, ask one or more of the following:
- Which of you helped me transport something it's better not to ask about?
- Which of you knows someone who would like to see me in court?
- Which of you finds my knowledge valuable — but finds me personally a little unsettling?
- Which of you represents an interesting anatomical case to me?

## Starting Moves

**First Cut**
When you bring a dying person back from the edge (someone at 0 HP or below), roll +INT. On a 10+ you stabilise them — they regain 1 HP and will not deteriorate further. On a 7-9 you stabilise them, but the procedure demands a hard choice: it will take long enough that something else goes wrong, or the patient recovers conscious with a lasting side effect — the GM says what.

**Field Surgery**
When you have a moment's calm and basic tools, you can perform an operation: extract an arrowhead, suture a wound, amputate, remove infection. You always know what technically needs doing. When you carry out a difficult procedure in unfavourable conditions, roll +CON. On a 10+ the patient recovers full HP. On a 7-9 it works, but a complication follows — scarring, pain, weakness, or the need to return in a week.

**Diagnosis**
When you carefully examine a patient for a few minutes, you always learn: what is wrong with them, what will kill them if you don't act, and what is needed to save them. For further questions roll +INT; on a 10+ ask three, on a 7-9 ask one.

**Cold Blood**
Gore, viscera, death, and pain leave no impression on you. You are immune to Fear effects triggered by graphic wounds, torture, or death. When others lose their heads in panic, you can act calmly and take the initiative — if you do, everyone you assist may reroll with +1.

**Anatomical Knowledge**
You know exactly where it hurts most. When you attack someone with knowledge of their physiology (a creature or person you have had even a moment to observe), your damage ignores 1 armour. When someone deals damage to you, you may roll +INT — on a 10+ you halve it by avoiding the critical strikes.

## Advances

- **Surgical Specialisation:** When you perform field surgery, on a 10+ choose two effects instead of one; on a 7-9 you always avoid permanent complications.
- **Disease Resistant:** You are a difficult target for infection and illness. When exposed to disease or plague, roll +CON — on a 10+ you ignore it entirely, on a 7-9 you cut its course in half.
- **Apprentice:** A young student joins you, following simple orders, carrying equipment, and learning anatomy from wounds rather than textbooks. Treat them as a hireling from the Core Rules.
- **Guild Licence:** You have obtained — honestly or otherwise — an official medical guild licence. You may practise legally, charge fees, and draw on the Guild's network of contacts in any city of the Empire.
- **Cold Analysis:** When you observe an opponent before or during a fight, you may ask the GM one question about their physical weaknesses or tactical vulnerabilities and always receive an honest answer.
- **Improved Stat:** Increase one of your stats by +1, to a maximum of +2.
- **Greater Stat** *(Requires: Improved Stat and at least one other Advance)*: Increase one of your stats by +1, to a maximum of +3.

## Drives

- Document a medical case so thoroughly it becomes part of the textbooks — even if the Guild would rather it stayed quiet.
- Find a cure for the disease that took someone important from you.
- Gain access to the body of a creature or race whose anatomy you have never studied.
- Reach the place where medicine meets something science should not touch.
