# BURGHER

*"Burgher" is gender-neutral — this playbook works for any gender.*

*You know every alley, every trader, and every rumour in the city. The city is your element — you understand how the system works and how to make it work for you.*

**Damage Die:** d6
**HP:** 6 + CON

## Background

Pick one.

---

**Street Trader / Pedlar**

You started with a market stall or a goods cart. You learned to talk to anyone — nobleman, thief, and watchman alike — and sell each of them something they need. When you look for a buyer for goods or a service in an unfamiliar place, the GM tells you who is interested, at what price, and what complication may arise.

When you introduce yourself to the other PCs, ask one or more of the following:
- Which of you bought something from me at an inflated price and never complained?
- Which of you knows I have access to goods that are difficult to buy legally?
- Which of you once lent me money when business was bad?
- Which of you looks down on me — until they need a favour?

---

**City Official / Alderman**

You work or worked for the city — tax collector, scribe, councillor's assistant. You know procedures, regulations, and above all the people who handle the paperwork. When you draw on knowledge of local law, ordinances, or official procedures, the GM tells you how the system works and where the gaps are.

When you introduce yourself to the other PCs, ask one or more of the following:
- Which of you owes me for making a certain official matter disappear from the records?
- Which of you knows I have access to information I shouldn't have?
- Which of you thinks I'm a dull pen-pusher — and is wrong?
- Which of you could help me with a problem that falls outside my jurisdiction?

---

**Entrepreneur / Establishment Owner**

You have — or had — your own business: a tavern, a small shop, a stable, a service operation. You know how to keep accounts, manage people, and avoid being robbed. When you look for lodging, food, a hiding place, or an ally among local merchants, you always find someone who understands professional solidarity.

When you introduce yourself to the other PCs, ask one or more of the following:
- Which of you knows me from earlier days when business was worse?
- Which of you knows why I had to leave my establishment and take to the road?
- Which of you once worked for me — or wanted to?
- Which of you believes I'll eventually get back on my feet and run a proper operation again?

## Starting Moves

**Network of Contacts**
In every city or larger settlement you visit, you know someone, or you know someone who knows someone. When you search for information, a service, or a person in the city, roll +CHA. On a 10+ you find what you need, discreetly and without strings attached. On a 7-9 you find it, but either it takes time, you owe a favour, or someone unwanted also hears about it — GM chooses.

**Reading the Room**
Years of watching people taught you when negotiations are going well and when a fight is about to break out. When you enter a new place or group and take a moment to look around, you may ask the GM one question; the GM must answer honestly:
- Who really matters here?
- Who wants something from the others?
- Where is the exit if things go wrong?

**Haggling and Appraisal**
You know what something is worth — really worth, not what someone is asking for it. When you buy, sell, or appraise goods, roll +INT. On a 10+ you get the best possible price and learn one flaw or hidden value in the deal. On a 7-9 the transaction goes through, but the price is a compromise.

**City Survival**
The city is not safe, but you know its rules. When you flee, hide, or cover your tracks in an urban environment, roll +DEX. On a 10+ you vanish without a trace. On a 7-9 you escape, but you leave something or someone behind — the GM will say what.

**Versatile**
Years of working different trades have had their effect. Choose two skills from the list below — you can use them like a professional without rolling, as long as circumstances are favourable:
- Dealing with customers and resolving disputes
- Handling horses and horse-drawn vehicles
- Gambling and money games
- Basic accounting and reading documents
- Knowledge of local laws and customs

## Advances

- **Shadow Mayor:** Your network grows. When you search for a contact, you can choose: someone discreet, someone influential, or someone who doesn't ask questions.
- **Word of Honour:** When you give your word or make a verbal agreement, NPCs treat it as a binding contract. Breaking your word has concrete social consequences — for both parties.
- **Grease the Wheels:** When you try to bribe an official, guard, or merchant, you always know how much you need and what beyond money might work.
- **Fits In Anywhere:** You can pose as someone else — a pedlar, a servant, a minor nobleman — convincingly enough that only close and sustained scrutiny exposes the deception.
- **Local Power:** In one city or region you are genuinely known. You have access there to information, services, and protected locations without rolling. Outside that area, your reputation counts for nothing.
- **Improved Stat:** Increase one of your stats by +1, to a maximum of +2.
- **Greater Stat** *(Requires: Improved Stat and at least one other Advance)*: Increase one of your stats by +1, to a maximum of +3.

## Drives

- Amass enough wealth that no one ever looks down on you again.
- Secure a seat on the town council — or a position behind it.
- Repay a debt to someone who helped you get back on your feet when you had nothing.
- Return home as someone important, not as who you were before you left.
