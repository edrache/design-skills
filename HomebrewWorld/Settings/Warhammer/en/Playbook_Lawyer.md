## Background

Pick one.

---

**Guild Advocate**

You represent guilds, merchants, and wealthy patrons — those who can afford your services and pay on time. You know trade law, guild charters, and contracts better than anyone else at the table. When you examine an agreement, contract, or arrangement, the GM tells you: what the terms actually mean, what they make possible — and where someone hid a trap.

When you introduce yourself to the other PCs, ask one or more of the following:
- Which of you is my client, or wants to be?
- Which of you has a contract or obligation they haven't mentioned to the group?
- Which of you knows whose interests I'm really representing, and is keeping that to themselves?
- Which of you is on the same side as me right now — though neither of us has admitted it yet?

---

**Defender of the Poor**

You defend those who cannot afford a lawyer — the destitute, the wrongly accused, those the system overlooks. You often work in opposition to authority, sometimes for nothing, out of vocation or religious duty. Your methods are unorthodox but effective. When you help someone with no resources or influence fight the system, your words always carry weight — even before a judge who's been bought.

When you introduce yourself to the other PCs, ask one or more of the following:
- Which of you was once my client — or still is?
- Which of you thinks my ideals are naive, but respects me anyway?
- Which of you is hiding something I might decide to turn into a case?
- Which of you knows that the enemy I have sits higher than any of us?

---

**Underworld Counsel**

You operate at the margins of legality — serving thieves' guilds, gaming houses, organizations no one mentions by name in public. Your clients pay well and ask few questions. You know loopholes, bribes, and the back entrance of every courthouse in the Empire. When you need to reach someone from the criminal underworld, a black market, or the lower depths of society, you always know who to approach — though they'll have their own requirements.

When you introduce yourself to the other PCs, ask one or more of the following:
- Which of you knows I can make certain problems disappear — for a price?
- Which of you has a past that would make introductions to my clients easier?
- Which of you once used my services, and we never quite settled accounts?
- Which of you doesn't ask for details — and why does that suit me perfectly?

## Starting Moves

**Knowledge of the Law**
When you ask how the law applies to a given situation — local, Imperial, guild, or ecclesiastical — the GM must tell you the truth. You may ask whether something is legal, whether any statute helps or hinders you, and what the penalties for a violation would be. If a rule exists, you know it.

**Legal Loophole**
When you're in trouble because of a law, regulation, or bureaucratic obstruction, roll +INT. On a 10+ you identify a gap, a precedent, or an interpretation that gets you out without cost. On a 7-9 you get out, but the GM chooses one: it takes time, it creates a debt, or it closes one door for the future.

**Closing Argument**
When you address a court, a crowd, a council, or someone in authority, roll +CHA. On a 10+ you persuade them to your position and may demand one specific concession. On a 7-9 you made an impression, but they need something from you first — the GM says what.

**Network of Contacts**
Years of practice taught you that lawyers don't win alone — they win through the people they know. When you need someone with specific skills, information, or access, you can always reach such a person. The GM will tell you what they want in return.

**Cold Assessment**
When you enter a new social, political, or judicial situation, you may ask the GM: who actually holds power here, what do they really want, and what might make them cooperative. The GM answers honestly as many questions as you have time to ask before you act.

## Advances

- **Specialisation: Criminal Law:** When you prosecute or defend someone before a court on a criminal matter, your Closing Argument succeeds as a 10+ even on a 7-9 roll — provided you have at least one piece of evidence or a witness.
- **Specialisation: Commercial Law:** When you analyse a contract or negotiate financial terms, you always secure at least one condition more favourable than the other party intended to offer.
- **Apprentice:** A young law student joins you, carrying files, copying documents, and learning the trade. Treat them as a hireling from the Core Rules.
- **Archive:** You own or have standing access to a legal archive — precedents, registries, rulings. When you search for a precedent or case history, you always find it — if anyone ever recorded it.
- **Political Influence:** Your reputation has reached people with real authority. Once per session you may invoke your name to delay an official action by one day, or to gain an audience you would not normally be granted.
- **Commanding Presence:** When you stand before someone and speak with authority, treat results of 7-9 on Closing Argument as a 10+ — but only when you are right about the law.
- **Improved Stat:** Increase one of your stats by +1, to a maximum of +2.
- **Greater Stat** *(Requires: Improved Stat and at least one other Advance)*: Increase one of your stats by +1, to a maximum of +3.

## Drives

- Bring to conviction someone who has been buying impunity for years.
- Prove the innocence of someone the system has already condemned.
- Build a reputation great enough that the Lawyers' Guild can neither ignore nor control you.
- Repay a debt — financial, legal, or moral — to someone who once gave you your chance.
