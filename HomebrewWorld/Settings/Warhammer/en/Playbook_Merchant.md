## Background

Pick one.

---

**Caravan Trader**

You know every road between the Empire's cities and every inn where you can safely spend the night with a chest of gold. Your wealth travels on wheels. When you seek information about roads, local dangers, or a safe passage, you always know who to ask — and you have something to pay them with.

When you introduce yourself to the other PCs, ask one or more of the following:
- Which of you once traveled in my caravan — as a passenger or as a guard?
- Which of you knows I once transported something I'd rather not talk about?
- Which of you has a contact that could help me on the eastern road?
- Which of you saved my cargo — or stole it and returned it?

---

**City Merchant (Guild)**

You have premises, a license, and regular customers. The Guild gives you protection and access to its network — but also obligations and enemies within the chapter. When you enter a city where the Merchants' Guild operates, you can invoke your membership to find a roof over your head, intelligence on the local market, and entry into merchant social circles.

When you introduce yourself to the other PCs, ask one or more of the following:
- Which of you has seen my Guild License and suspects it might be forged?
- Which of you has brokered a deal for me that the Guild might call irregular?
- Which of you is bound to me by a debt — financial or in favors?
- Which of you would one day like access to the circles I move in?

---

**Far-Trade Traveler**

You have been to places other merchants only hear about in rumors. Exotic goods, distant ports, foreign languages, foreign customs — your wealth comes from roads others consider too dangerous. When you seek a rare commodity, an exotic ingredient, or someone from a distant land, you know where to look and how to speak with people from outside the Empire.

When you introduce yourself to the other PCs, ask one or more of the following:
- Which of you saw me somewhere I shouldn't have been?
- Which of you knows what I brought back from my last journey — and why it's dangerous?
- Which of you speaks a language I only know poorly and could help me translate?
- Which of you thinks my stories about far-off lands are exaggerated?

## Starting Moves

**Appraise**
When you examine an item, commodity, or estate, you always know what it is worth and whether the seller is trying to cheat you. Roll +INT to learn something more: on a 10+ ask three questions from the list, on a 7-9 ask one.
- Where does this come from?
- Who else might want to buy this?
- Is there a hidden flaw or secret here?
- What is the best price I can realistically get?

**Gold Speaks**
Money solves most problems. When you offer payment, a bribe, or a fee for services, roll +CHA. On a 10+ you get what you want — no questions asked, no complications. On a 7-9 you get what you want, but the GM picks one: the price is higher than you expected, someone notices and will remember it, or you're buying loyalty only until a better offer comes along.

**Network of Contacts**
Your business requires connections. In every city or large settlement, you have at least one contact — a trader, official, innkeeper, or someone who owes you a favor. When you seek information, shelter, or an introduction to local circles, the GM tells you who your contact is here and what they want from you.

**Negotiator**
You are a natural at the bargaining table. When you haggle over a price, terms, or an arrangement, roll +CHA. On a 10+ you secure terms clearly in your favor. On a 7-9 you get a fair deal, but the other party expects something from you in return — the GM will say what.

**Financial Resources**
You don't carry your whole fortune on your person, but you know how to access money quickly. When you need coin for something that can be bought legally, you can get it — the question is only how fast and what you must sacrifice. The GM will present the available options and their costs.

## Advances

- **Bribe-Giver:** When you bribe an official, guard, or member of an organization, roll +CHA. On a 10+ it works flawlessly and the recipient is your ally for the duration of the matter. On a 7-9 it works, but someone else in that organization will find out.
- **Warehouse:** You own or have access to a secure storage location. You can hide goods, people, money, or secrets there — the GM will say what threatens it if anyone comes looking.
- **Embezzler:** When you manipulate documents, contracts, or ledgers, you can always do so in a way that cannot be immediately detected. When someone scrutinizes them carefully — roll +INT.
- **Broker:** When you connect two parties who want to buy or sell something, you always profit from the transaction — even if both sides believe they got a fair deal.
- **Caravan Purse:** Your financial resources run deeper than anyone suspects. When you need a substantial sum in an emergency, once per session you can simply have it — no roll required, but with consequences the GM will reveal later.
- **Improved Stat:** Increase one of your stats by +1, to a maximum of +2.
- **Greater Stat** *(Requires: Improved Stat and at least one other Advance)*: Increase one of your stats by +1, to a maximum of +3.

## Drives

- Accumulate enough wealth to open your own trading house and free yourself from the Guild.
- Repay a debt — financial or moral — to someone who gave you your first capital.
- Find a trade route or commodity that will make your fortune before a competitor discovers it.
- Prove that money can open doors the nobility considers exclusively their own.
