## Background

Pick one.

---

**Private Investigator**

You work for gold — for anyone who pays. A merchant's missing wife, a stolen list of creditors, a silent killer in an alley. You don't ask for reasons until the case stops being worth the risk. When someone hires you to solve a problem and gives you a starting point, the GM reveals one non-obvious fact about the case that no one else would have noticed.

When you introduce yourself to the other PCs, ask one or more of the following:
- Which of you once hired me for a case you didn't tell me the whole truth about?
- Which of you knows I owe a debt to someone not known for patience?
- Which of you was a party in a case I was working — not on your side?
- Which of you thinks I do this for the money, not because I can't sleep otherwise?

---

**Institutional Agent**

You serve a patron — a merchant guild, the Church of Sigmar, or the city watch. You have access to resources private investigators can only envy: archives, trusted informants, seals authorizing searches. When you invoke your institution's authority to gain access to a place, document, or person, roll +CHA. On a 10+ you get access without questions. On a 7-9 you get access, but someone records it — the GM will say where that information ends up.

When you introduce yourself to the other PCs, ask one or more of the following:
- Which of you knows someone who dealt with me professionally and didn't come out well?
- Which of you knows my institution has its own goals — and they don't always match mine?
- Which of you needs something I have access to, but that I can't give freely?
- Which of you was watching me before we met?

---

**Investigative Journalist**

You write for a newspaper — and every truth you expose earns you another enemy. Merchant corruption, a cult hidden beneath a monastery, a poisoner in a palace. Your pen is more dangerous than a sword, and everyone knows it. When you publicly expose a scandal or the truth about an influential person, roll +INT. On a 10+ the story achieves its intended effect and your sources remain safe. On a 7-9 the story runs, but someone loses more because of you than you intended — the GM decides who and what.

When you introduce yourself to the other PCs, ask one or more of the following:
- Which of you read my articles and changed their mind about someone important?
- Which of you has a secret I'd want to write about, and knows it?
- Which of you protected me when someone tried to silence me permanently?
- Which of you believes that in some matters, it's better not to dig?

## Starting Moves

**Crime Scene Analysis**
When you examine a scene — a crime, an accident, a mystery — before it is disturbed, ask the GM up to three questions from the list below. The GM must answer honestly.
- What actually happened here?
- Who was here before us, and when?
- What was someone trying to hide?
- What does this event say about who is behind it?
- Is there something here that's been overlooked at first glance?

**Investigation**
When you actively search for information — through conversation, observation, or searching — roll +INT. On a 10+ the GM reveals three specific, true pieces of information about the case. On a 7-9 the GM reveals one piece of information, but your questions attract attention — the GM will say whose.

**Read a Liar**
When someone tells you something that may not be true, roll +WIS. On a 10+ the GM tells you whether they're lying, what they're lying about, and what they're actually trying to hide. On a 7-9 the GM tells you only whether they're lying.

**Breaking and Entering**
When you attempt to enter a locked place without permission, roll +DEX. On a 10+ you get in without a trace and without losing time. On a 7-9 you get in, but the GM picks one: you leave a trace, it takes too long, or something is left behind.

**Informant Network**
You have a network of contacts in the city — bribable officials, market gossips, night watchmen, thieves who know their territory. When you search for someone or something in the city, you always know who to approach. The GM will tell you what that person will want in return.

## Advances

- **Penetrating Interrogation:** When you interrogate someone for at least several minutes, the GM must answer one of your questions honestly — regardless of any roll. You may ask further questions by rolling +CHA.
- **Photographic Memory:** When you observe a scene, document, or face, you remember every detail forever. You may ask the GM one question about something you previously saw, and the GM must answer.
- **Connections in Dark Places:** Your informant network reaches beyond the legal world. When you search for information about gangs, smugglers, or cults, treat it as a normal Investigation — without any additional risk.
- **Difficult Target:** You are exceptionally hard to track. When someone tries to surveil you or get on your trail, roll +DEX. On a 10+ you disappear from their sight entirely. On a 7-9 you lose them, but it costs you time.
- **Assistant:** A helper joins you — a young apprentice or a runner — who follows orders, gathers information, and carries messages. Treat them as a hireling from the Core Rules.
- **Improved Stat:** Increase one of your stats by +1, to a maximum of +2.
- **Greater Stat** *(Requires: Improved Stat and at least one other Advance)*: Increase one of your stats by +1, to a maximum of +3.

## Drives

- Uncover the truth about a case that someone very powerful had buried.
- Prove that your methods work even when everyone around you has already given up.
- Repay a debt to an informant who once risked their life for you.
- Build a reputation great enough that the City Watch comes to you — not the other way around.
