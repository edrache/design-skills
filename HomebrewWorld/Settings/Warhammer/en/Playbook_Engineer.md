## Background

Pick one.

---

**Guild Constructor**

You completed formal training with the Engineers Guild — workshops, calculations, masters in polished uniforms. Your license is genuine and paid for. You know the protocols, the certifications, and the right people. When you build or repair a machine with access to tools and a workshop, the result is always professional and lasting — the GM will tell you how long it takes and what you need, but never questions the outcome.

When you introduce yourself to the other PCs, ask one or more of the following:
- Which of you witnessed my earlier work and knows the explosion wasn't an accident?
- Which of you knows someone in the Guild who considers me a threat?
- Which of you once needed something built and preferred not to ask the Guild?
- Which of you knows that my license covers something that should be illegal?

---

**Experimental Inventor**

The Guild is a noose around the neck of creativity. Your designs are original, your results — spectacular in both possible directions. Explosions are part of the research process. When you improvise a device or technical solution from available materials, roll +INT. On a 10+ it works exactly as intended. On a 7-9 it works — but with one unforeseen side effect, which the GM describes.

When you introduce yourself to the other PCs, ask one or more of the following:
- Which of you was present at one of my failed tests and survived?
- Which of you helped me obtain materials without asking what they were for?
- Which of you feels uneasy whenever I tinker with anything nearby?
- Which of you was impressed — and is still hiding it?

---

**Sapper Specialist**

You served at cannons, catapults, and siege engines. You know how to bring down walls, blow open gates, and direct ballistic fire. Combat is an engineering problem. When you prepare a battlefield, defensive position, or ambush using terrain and tools, you always gain one concrete tactical advantage — the GM tells you what it is before the action begins.

When you introduce yourself to the other PCs, ask one or more of the following:
- Which of you fought beside my machine and knows what I did at the end of that battle?
- Which of you thinks I was on the wrong side of the conflict I served in?
- Which of you trusted me in the field and doesn't regret it?
- Which of you knows I deserted the army — and why?

## Starting Moves

**Workshop on Your Back**
Your kit contains basic tools and materials. When you repair a broken device, weapon, or vehicle, you always know what is wrong and how to fix it — the only question is whether you have the parts and time. When you repair something in the field, roll +INT. On a 10+ you repair it completely. On a 7-9 you restore function, but with one limitation or temporary fix that will fail before long.

**Black Powder Arms**
Pistols, muskets, blunderbusses — you know them from the inside. When you fire a black powder weapon at a chosen target and have time to aim, roll +DEX. On a 10+ you hit and deal full damage. On a 7-9 you hit, but choose one: either you use the last of your powder, or the noise draws unwanted attention.

**Read the Plans**
When you study blueprints, schematics, or technical drawings — even damaged, incomplete, or in an unknown language — roll +INT. On a 10+ you understand it fully: its purpose, weak points, and hidden mechanisms. On a 7-9 you grasp the general purpose, but one key question remains unanswered — ask the GM.

**Hard Head**
Years in forges, under the thunder of cannons, and in the smoke of black powder have their effect. When you try to keep a clear head in chaos — explosion, crash, structural collapse — roll +CON. On a 10+ you act without impairment. On a 7-9 you hold it together, but take 1d4 damage or lose one important tool.

**Assess the Structure**
When you evaluate a building, wall, bridge, or fortification, you always know: how much stress it can take, where it is weakest, and what you would need to bring it down or reinforce it. The GM tells you this without a roll.

## Advances

- **Controlled Detonation:** When you use explosives or black powder charges, you can designate the precise point of detonation and minimize collateral damage — or maximize it deliberately. The effect is always exactly as you intended.
- **Experimental Weapon:** You build one unique weapon or combat device. Describe how it works; the GM describes what makes it exceptionally effective — and the one flaw that means no guild will ever certify it.
- **Battle Wagon:** You have access to a modified vehicle — a carriage, cart, or gun platform — treat it as a hireling from the Core Rules with the traits: Armor +1 and a Special Ability you choose.
- **Masterwork:** Spend time and materials to create the work of your life — a bridge, machine, weapon, or fortress. You and the GM describe the result together; it is not subject to accidental damage.
- **Apprentices:** Two young students join you as assistants. Treat them as hirelings from the Core Rules; one of them always breaks something, but the other is genuinely gifted.
- **Improved Stat:** Increase one of your stats by +1, to a maximum of +2.
- **Greater Stat** *(Requires: Improved Stat and at least one other Advance)*: Increase one of your stats by +1, to a maximum of +3.

## Drives

- Build a machine that changes how people think about travel, war, or construction.
- Prove to the Guild — or its ruins — that you were right all along.
- Find the design someone buried because it was too dangerous for those who ruled at the time.
- Build something indestructible that outlasts you and everyone you know.
