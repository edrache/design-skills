## Background

Choose one.

---

**Academic Researcher**

You work within an institution — a university, library, or magical archive. You have a title, access to collections, and colleagues for debate. You also have a prior who checks your notes, a bursar who monitors expenses, and a rector who wants results. When you search a library, archive, or academic written source, you always find what you are looking for — if anyone ever wrote it down. The GM may tell you the information exists but lies elsewhere.

When you introduce yourself to the other Player Characters, ask one or more of the following:
- Which of you knows why I left the walls of the academy for matters less... bibliographical?
- Which of you once asked me for information and regretted receiving it?
- Which of you needs me to keep accurate records of this venture?
- Which of you thinks me naive — until they look at what I know?

---

**Seeker of Lost Knowledge**

The most valuable texts do not sit on university shelves — they lie in ruins, tombs, and secret archives. You travel the Old World following lost fragments, forbidden books, and legends that turn out to be true. When you examine ancient ruins, unknown artefacts, or forbidden texts, roll +INT. On a 10+ the GM tells you three things: what it is, where it comes from, and what it concerns. On a 7-9 you learn one thing and sense that someone or something now knows you were here.

When you introduce yourself to the other Player Characters, ask one or more of the following:
- Which of you travels with me for reasons other than curiosity?
- Which of you did I tell what I am searching for — and do I regret it?
- Which of you has already pulled me out of somewhere I should not have been?
- Which of you knows this particular knowledge may be too dangerous to find?

---

**Scribe and Chronicler**

You write letters for the illiterate, copy documents for notaries, translate edicts for village headmen. And in doing so, you hear everything. When you want to learn something about local affairs, people, or events by asking questions and listening, roll +CHA. On a 10+ the GM gives you three useful pieces of information about the situation. On a 7-9 you learn one thing, but someone knows you are asking.

When you introduce yourself to the other Player Characters, ask one or more of the following:
- Which of you once hired me to write something they would rather forget?
- Which of you knows I am keeping a chronicle of this venture — and wonders what I write in it?
- Which of you did I help in a matter that required someone literate and discreet?
- Which of you understands that what I document may one day change the course of history?

## Starting Moves

**Specialist Knowledge**
When you encounter something within your Field of Study — an artefact, text, phenomenon, creature, or historical event — you always know something about it that others do not. The GM tells you one concrete piece of information about what you see. If you want to know more, roll +INT. On a 10+ ask three questions from the list below; on a 7-9 ask one:
- Where does this come from and when?
- What does or did it do?
- Is there a danger here that cannot be seen?
- Who else knows about this?

**Pattern Recognition**
When you gather information from multiple sources — conversation, documents, observation — and try to draw conclusions, roll +INT. On a 10+ the GM tells you what is really happening: the hidden plan, the true culprit, or the forgotten connection. On a 7-9 the GM points you in the right direction, but further investigation will be required.

**Memory Palace**
Your memory is an archive. When you want to recall a detail from the past — anything you have ever read, heard, or witnessed — the GM tells you whether that information is in your store. If it is, you have it. If not, you know that you do not know — and where you might find out.

**Polyglot**
You know three languages beyond Reikspiel (choose from: Classical, Tilean, Estalian, Bretonnian, Khazalid, Eltharin, Arabyan, Norse, fragments of the Dark Tongue). When you encounter a written or spoken language you do not know, roll +INT. On a 10+ you understand it well enough to grasp its essential meaning. On a 7-9 you understand the general sense but may mistake something important — the GM decides what.

**Situational Assessment**
When you have a moment to observe and evaluate a place, group, or problem before acting, roll +WIS. On a 10+ ask two questions; the GM answers honestly:
- What is the greatest weakness or threat in this situation?
- What is an unexpected or non-obvious solution?
On a 7-9 ask one question.

## Advances

- **Second Field:** Choose an additional scholarly specialisation. You may apply Specialist Knowledge to both fields.
- **Academic Network:** You have contacts in institutions across the Old World. When you seek an expert, commission research, or need a letter of introduction, you always find the right person — a matter of time and the cost of correspondence.
- **Personal Archive:** You carry a systematic set of notes. When you use Memory Palace or Specialist Knowledge, you may also ask one additional question beyond the move's limit.
- **Student:** A young apprentice accompanies you, carrying notes, copying texts, and learning. Treat them as followers from the rulebook. They may independently use Specialist Knowledge in your field at the 7-9 level.
- **Accomplished Speaker:** When you explain something complex to common people or persuade someone through argument, roll +INT instead of +CHA.
- **Unexpected Resources:** Your knowledge is worth its weight in gold. When you offer information, expertise, or translation as payment, the GM treats it as full payment in coin. Once per session.
- **Improved Stat:** Increase one of your stats by +1, to a maximum of +2.
- **Higher Stat** *(Requires: Improved Stat and at least one other Advance)*: Increase one of your stats by +1, to a maximum of +3.

## Drives

- Find a lost text or artefact that will change the understanding of Old World history.
- Prove that your Field has practical significance — even to those who laughed at you while holding a sword.
- Record and preserve knowledge threatened by forgetting, destruction, or concealment.
- Find the answer to the question that destroyed your master — and do not let it destroy you.
