## Tags

Tags are short descriptive phrases that describe attributes of  your  character  or  the  setting.  Example  character  tags  could be golden gloves boxer , or insomniac , or my mom is an anthropology professor . Example setting tags (town tags, in the default setting) could be the corrupt police ,  or the statue of the town mascot ,  or the annual winter festival .

Tags represent things about the characters or setting that you want to see come into play. You build scenes out of tags, so you want all of the tags to be things you're interested in telling a story about.

Tags can be positive, negative, or wild. Your character will start with five positive, your choice of one, two, or three negative tags, and one wild tag. You'll also start with at least one bond, and the town will start with five town tags.

Positive tags  represent  your  character's  skills,  abilities, resources, and connections. They can only help you. You invoke them to get +1 on your roll when you Investigate or Face Peril.

Negative tags represent your character's weaknesses, ways the world is more difficult for you, and non-player characters who have a problem with you. They can only hinder you. The Oracle invokes them to give you -1 on your roll when you Investigate or  Face  Peril.  Whenever  they  do,  you  gain  a  humanity,  which you can use later on to use the Reveal Something About Yourself revelation.

Wild tags  represent  things  about  your  character  that sometimes help and sometimes hinder you, like aspects of their personality, equipment, or skills they can't quite rely on. They can be invoked as positive or negative tags.

Bonds are a special kind of wild tag. They represent the relationship  between  two  protagonists  -  how  they  know  each other, what they've done together before. Both protagonists have the same bond, and they can only invoke the tag if they're both in the same scene together.

Town tags  are  a  special  kind  of  wild  tag.  They  describe the town, not a specific character, and so they can be invoked by any character, as long as they make sense in the fiction. For other settings besides small towns, town tags are called setting tags .

Temporary tags  are  a  special  kind  of  wild  tag.  They represent something that only exists for a scene or two, like a minor injury, a gun you found with only a few bullets, or any other temporary resource or impediment. A temporary tag goes away when the Oracle or a move says to mark it off, typically after one or two scenes. Most often a temporary tag is helpful a few times and then harmful once and then gets marked off, or harmful a few times and then helpful once and then gets marked off, or just harmful once or twice.

Important  NPCs,  with  the  possible  exception  of  the antagonist, are going to be represented by a tag. It's important that each character, location, etc only be represented by a single tag. If they're someone you have a strong personal connection to, they'll be a tag that your character has, usually a positive tag. If they're a townsperson that everyone knows more or less equally, they'll be a town tag.

When you  use  a  tag  on  Investigate  or  Face  Peril,  that's called invoking it. When a player invokes a positive or wild tag, they get +1 to their roll. When the Oracle invokes a negative or wild tag, the player gets -1 to their roll, and gains a humanity. What tags you invoke determine which fictional elements come into play in the scene. Players can invoke at most 3 tags on a roll, and the Oracle can invoke at most 2.
