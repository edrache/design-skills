## Marking Off Options

Frequently on moves you'll notice options that look

- O like this, with a circle
- or like this, with a dot

The ones that have a dot you can pick whenever you have to choose from that list. They never get marked off.

The ones that have a circle will say 'Mark this off'. When you pick that option, you have to check off (or fill in, or put an X through, or otherwise mark) that circle, and you can't select that option again until you recover it . This means that your options go down as you continue investigating, facing peril, encountering true horror, and making revelations.

Most moves give you a way of recovering their options, but not the 7-9 options for Face Peril: there, you're always moving closer to death. For the others, the recovery option, which always has a dot, involves something going wrong for your character or the town, and are a good opportunity to roleplay the trauma and negative consequences of living throughwhat's happening to you.

Sometimes you'll be asked to mark off a tag. This means drawing a line through it, so that you can't use it anymore unless you recover it or gain it again.

## Hard Mode

Normally,  each  protagonist  uses  their  own  copy  of  the moves, so that one person marking things off doesn't affect what others have access to. To make the game more difficult and more dangerous, have the protagonists share a single copy of one or more of the moves among themselves.

## Assisting Someone

You can assist another protagonist when you're in the same scene as them and they make the Investigate or Face Peril moves, and it would make sense in the fiction for you to be able to help them.

You invoke one of your positive or wild tags, your mutual bond, or a town tag, and you give them +1 on their roll.

This counts as one of the 3 tags they can invoke, so you can't  raise  their  maximum  bonus  above  +3  when  you  do  this. Each person assisting them can only add 1 tag to their roll.

If you assist someone on an Investigation , then if they hit (get a 7+), you also get a clue: you can ask a follow up question to after they ask their initial question. However, you also expose yourself to potential danger if they get a 6 or lower.

If  you  assist  someone  when  they  Face  Peril,  you  don't gain any direct benefit the way you do when helping someone Investigate, and you're exposing yourself to even more danger than when you help someone Investigate. You'll have to decide if that's worth it to try to save their life!

If you assist someone, the Oracle can invoke your negative tags, as well as your bond with whoever you're helping and your wild tags, just like if you were the one making the roll. If the Oracle invokes a negative tag ,  including  invoking a wild tag, bond, or town tag on a roll where people are assisting, then both the protagonist making the roll and everyone assisting them gains  a  humanity .  If  the  protagonist  rolls  a  6-,  then  they  and everyone who assisted them gains a foreboding.

You can't help someone when they Encounter True Horror -  everyone  has  to  face  their  fears  on  their  own.  You  also  can't assist on any revelations.

## The Basic Moves

The basic moves are the moves you'll be doing the most often. They represent kinds of scenes, or important things that can happen in scenes, that you'll be playing through. For all of these, you'll roll two 6 sided dice and add something, but what you add can vary from move to move.

There are three basic moves: Investigate , Face Peril ,  and Encounter True Horror .

Investigate and Face Peril both use your tags. You'll pick up to three positive or wild tags your character has (or town tags!) to assist you. Each one gives you +1 to your roll. The Oracle will also pick up to 2 negative or wild tags that your character has (or town tags) and each of those will give you -1 to your roll, and a humanity.

Remember that each of your bonds is a wild tag, which you or the Oracle could invoke on a roll, if the other protagonist with that bond is in the scene.

You Investigate to find clues and figure things out - most commonly, you'll have a question you want to ask, some tags you want to use to figure it out, and then you'll construct a scene out of the tags used, the outcome of the roll, and whatever decisions you (or the Oracle) make based on that outcome.

You Face Peril when you try to survive danger. Any time that  you  could  plausibly  die,  you  have  to  Face  Peril.  If  you're planning ahead, you can set up a scene where you know you'll have to Face Peril, otherwise it might be sprung on you in the middle of a scene, and could determine the outcome of that scene - or you could have to Face Peril multiple times, and the scene might continue after the roll is resolved.

You Encounter True Horror whenever you're in a truly horrifying situation - seeing a dead body for the first time, being chased  by  a  killer,  learning  something  man  was  not  meant  to know, et cetera. Encounter True Horror is different from the other two basic moves in that your tags don't affect the roll - instead, you add your current total horror to the roll (and then after the roll, you gain 1 horror).

## Investigate

Whenever  you  Investigate  to  find  a  clue  to  a  question you're wondering about, roll plus tags . Rolling higher is better.

You Investigate whenever there's a question you want to know the answer to and you want to use your character's tags or  the  town  tags  to  try  to  figure  it  out.  Whether  you  ask  the members of your gardening club to try to identify a poison, or use your keen eyes to spot any physical evidence left behind, or find rumours among the workers down at the docks , or use any other combination of the fictional elements you've established to build a scene where you might find out the answer to your question, you're Investigating.

On a 7+ you find a clue relevant to the question you were asking. This could be the answer, or it could be learning where you can find more information, or anything else that leads you towards the answer. If you get more than one clue, you can use those  questions  to  follow  up  on  your  first  clue.  Anyone  who assisted you gets to ask a follow-up question as well.

The result of your Investigate roll will help you create an entire scene. Later Investigation rolls should typically create new scenes.

10+ In addition to the clue you get for hitting, you may choose one.

- O You find  something  useful.  You  gain  a  wild  tag,  such  as a shotgun, or blackmail on that obnoxious dilettante , or a fragment of an Elder Sign . Mark this off.
- O You find an additional clue. Ask a follow up question, or a question about something else. Mark this off.
- Someone  notices  your  investigation,  and  takes  an  interest. Recover the other two options.

- 7-9 In addition to the clue you get for hitting, you may choose one.
- O You find an additional clue, relevant to the question you were asking. Ask a follow up question, then mark this off.
- O You find something useful during your investigation. You gain a temporary tag, like the killer's weapon , or keys to... something , or the cultist's book . Mark this off.
- While  you're  investigating,  something  significant  happens elsewhere. Recover the other two options.
- 6- You gain foreboding and the Oracle chooses one or more of the following options.
- Your  negative  tags  that  were  invoked  come  into  play  in  a major way.
- You  can  still  discover  something,  but  something  else  goes wrong.
- You gain a temporary tag. Work with the Oracle to determine what it is.
- You find a clue that's unrelated to what you were looking for.
- You learn a horrible secret that tests your mettle, and have to Encounter True Horror.
- Someone else learns something about you that you'd rather they didn't.
- You end up in a treacherous situation, and likely have to Face Peril.
- Someone who assisted you must suffer in some way.
- Something bad happens to a town tag that was invoked.
- An NPC in the scene gets in some sort of trouble.
- Something bad happens elsewhere while you're distracted.
- Something goes very wrong, and you have to mark off a tag.
- Some other devious scenario of the Oracle's invention.

## Face Peril

Whenever you are in a potentially  deadly  situation  and must Face Peril to survive, roll plus tags . Rolling higher is better.

You Face Peril whenever you're attacked by an antagonist, be they monster or man, or you confront them yourself, are in a dangerous environment, or any other time you have to face a threat that plausibly might kill you.  Whether you use your fencing to try to stop the maniac with a machete, your football skills to try to leap across the hole in the floor of the crumbling mansion, or your experience as a sailor to  undo the knots holding you and escape before your kidnapper returns, you're Facing Peril.

Combat is frequently a single Face Peril roll, or a single roll per protagonist. Typically, the results of your Face Peril roll will determine how the scene plays out, though in a particularly deadly game you may need to Face Peril more than once in a single scene.

## 10+ Choose one.

- O Your experience, and your success at it, affects you for the better. You gain a positive tag, such as a sense of calm , or a sharp eye , or even (with the Oracle's approval) I know what hurts them . Mark this off.
- O You find something that gets you out of the situation safely. You gain a positive tag, such as an old camp axe ,  or a cultist's decoder ring , or a truck . Mark this off.
- O You make or reveal a connection with an NPC who helps you out. Gain a wild tag naming them, such as my study partner Szandor , or my childhood bestie Rebecca , or 'Laces' the hobo . Mark this off.
- You  succeed,  but  barely,  at  the  skin  of  your  teeth.  It  costs you something. Mark off a positive or wild tag, or a town tag that was invoked on this roll. Recover one of the other three options.

## 7-9 Choose one.

- O Suffer a minor injury - lose a temporary tag you invoked in this scene, or gain a temporary tag, like I smacked my hand , or I'm feeling dizzy , or I lost my glasses . Mark this off.
- O Suffer a chronic injury - gain a negative tag, like horrific scar , or missing an ear , or permanent limp . Mark this off.
- O Suffer a crippling injury - gain a negative tag, like broken leg , or missing eye , or lost a hand . If you don't receive medical attention soon, choose an additional result on this list. If you do, replace the negative tag with a wild tag, like uses a sturdy silver-topped cane ,  or wears a mesmerizing glass eye ,  or has an artificial hand . Mark this off.
- You manage to escape mostly unscathed, but something in town doesn't. Mark off a town tag you or the Oracle invoked in this scene.
- O You die. Work with the Oracle to describe how you die.
- 6- You gain foreboding and the Oracle chooses one or more of the following options.
- Your  negative  tags  that  were  invoked  come  into  play  in  a major way.
- You die. This should only happen in particularly dangerous or foolhardy situations, or in the climax.
- You mark off one or more 7-9 options.

- You find yourself in a yet more dangerous position.
- Something truly horrible happens, and you have to Encounter True Horror.

- Something bad happens to a town tag that was invoked.
- An NPC in the scene gets hurt, or worse.

## Encounter True Horror

Whenever  you  Encounter  True  Horror  and  need  to  see how well you cope with it, roll plus horror. Whatever the result, you gain a horror after the roll. Rolling higher is worse.

You  Encounter  True  Horror  whenever  you  see  a  scene of violence beyond anything you've encountered before, when you  see something beyond your understanding, when you learn something man was not meant to know, or when you're in any other situation that you or the Oracle decide would horrify your character.

## 13+ Choose one of the following.

- O You lose yourself in the horror. Perhaps you find something compelling in it and switch allegiances, or perhaps you retreat into your mind and never leave the fetal position again, or perhaps you retreat to your room to write a mysterious tome for the rest of your life. Mark this off.
- You're barely holding on. Mark off a positive tag or a town tag. Perhaps you're too traumatized to use a particular skill again,  or  perhaps  later  tonight  you  sleepwalk  and  burn down the town mall, or perhaps you lash out and break your favorite tool in the process.

## 10-12 Choose one of the following.

- O You take  foolish  or  selfish  action,  potentially  endangering yourself and/or other people around you. Perhaps you rush at a monster you have no hope of defeating, or perhaps you read the spell you just discovered aloud from the creepy old book you just found before anyone can stop you, or perhaps

you simply attempt to flee in a way that could make it harder for your friends to follow. Mark this off.

- O You experience the sudden shock to your system in a way that permanently alters your sense of self. Gain a wild tag, like prophetic  nightmares ,  or foolhardy  bravery ,  or obsessive paranoia . Mark this off.
- Your experiences are wearing on you. Gain a negative tag, like pareidolia ( seeing patterns that aren ' t  there ) ,  or losing moments of  time ,  or  another  trauma  symptom  or  mental  illness  that you as the player want to explore with your character, and which your fellow players and the Oracle are comfortable with having in the game. Recover the other two options.
- 7-9 Choose one of the following.
- O You  notice  something  useful  in  the  fear,  and  gain  a  clue. Perhaps you spot a potential weakness in the creature that just surprised you, or notice a useful detail in the instructions for the spell in the tome you just read, or piece together a pattern you hadn ' t noticed before. Ask a question about what you've encountered and mark this off.
- O You spot an opportunity, and put either yourself or someone else in danger to take advantage of it. Perhaps the spell will require  a  sacrifice,  and  you  don ' t  tell  one  of  your  fellow casters that, or perhaps you can leap in between your allies and the creature to aid their escape, or perhaps you knock one of them down to aid your own. Mark this off.
- Your trauma is starting to pile up. Gain an additional horror. Recover the other two options.

6- You gain foreboding, and aside from becoming slightly more horrified, nothing else bad happens - yet.

## Revelations

As you play, you'll pick up the three forms of experience: Horror (which  you  gain  when  you  Encounter  True  Horror), Humanity (which you gain when your negative tags are invoked), and Foreboding (which you gain when you roll a 6 or below on a move). Revelations are how you use that experience.

Revelations are moves that don't require any dice rolling -  you  just  decide  to  use  them  when  you  have  enough  of  the appropriate form of experience. They create a short scene where you develop your character or the world .

This scene could be a flashback, where you reveal something that's been the case this whole time and just hasn't come up yet, or they could be a short interlude where your character or the world changes, or they could be a montage, or a sudden burst of inspiration or change of heart, or even an extended scene that involves multiple characters and goes on for a while. The point is that they either flesh out the world further or progress your protagonist's character arc.

The first revelation is Let the Horror Sink In . This lets you spend 3 horror, so you don't have to keep adding that to your Encounter  True  Horror  rolls,  but  in  exchange,  something  bad has to happen. This represents the trauma of your experiences coming to bear and giving you long term effects.

The second revelation is Reveal Something  About Yourself . This costs 3 humanity, and represents either revealing more about your backstory  or  progressing  your  character  arc, and lets your protagonist improve in some way.

The  third  revelation  is Reveal  Something  About  the World .  This costs 3 foreboding, and represents either revealing something preexisting about the world that hasn't come up yet, or a change to the world.

The  final  revelation  is  the Epilogue .  This  calls  to  mind the  most  emotionally  significant  moment  in  the  story,  to  help with the Peak-End effect (see page 145) and then also lets you make as many other revelations as you'd like. These revelations represent small scenes at the end of the story that show how your characters and the town change after the end of the story.

## Let the Horror Sink In

When what you've been experiencing really gets to you and you start to experience trauma or otherwise let the horror sink in, you can spend three horror and gain one of the following effects.

- O You can no longer rely on something you used to depend on. What was it? How will you still make partial use of it, or its replacement, going forward? Mark off a positive tag. Gain a related wild tag. Mark this off.
- O Your experiences leave you shaken and with new problems. What are they? Gain a negative tag. Mark this off.
- Your experiences are making you lose part of yourself. Which part, and why? Mark off a positive tag. Recover the other two options.

## Reveal Something About the World

When you reveal something new about the world - which could be something truly new, or something that already existed that just hasn't been in the story yet - you can spend three foreboding and gain one of the following effects.

- There's something new in town - perhaps a new landmark, organization, or prominent player in town. Who or what is it? A biker gang that just rode into town, a new bookstore that opened on Main Street, a famous scientist who just joined the university? The town gains a tag naming them or it.
- Someone you know comes to town. Who are they? A cousin from the country, a colleague returning from an expedition, a  touring  musician  you  grew  up  with?  What  is  your relationship like? Gain a wild tag naming them.

- Some new and interesting item arrives on the scene. What is it? A rare tome at the University library, a strange statue at the art gallery, a mysterious machine in a warehouse by the docks? Gain a wild tag naming it - but you can't invoke it as a positive tag until you get your hands on it.

## reveal something about Yourself

When you reveal more about your backstory or make a decision about how your character arc should progress, you can spend three humanity and gain one of the following effects

- O You heal from your wounds. How do you do so? Emergency surgery  in  a  hospital,  convalescing  at  home,  black  market opiates? Recover a marked off option on face peril. Mark this off.
- We see an aspect of your character we haven't seen before, either  because  it  was  just  revealed,  or  because  it's  brand new. What is it? A flashback, a training montage, a shopping spree? Gain a positive tag representing it.
- You're working on overcoming your weaknesses. Which one are you currently working on, and how are you improving? Get a cane to help with your chronic limp, go to therapy to rein in your neuroses, pay off a debt?  Mark off a negative tag, and gain a related wild tag.
- You're feeling more  connected  to one of your fellow protagonists. Which one, and why? Gain a bond with them.

## Epilogue

At the end of a story, which should typically be at the end of a session (most stories should only last one session), have a series of short scenes showing how the characters and setting change after  the  events  of  the  story.  Think  of  a  denoument,  possibly followed by a  montage during the end credits.

First, ask all the players this together.

- Is there a particular person, location, organization, or something  else  that  you  encountered  tonight  that  you're certain will be more influential in the town going forward? If all of the players agree on the same answer, gain a town tag representing it.

Then, each player can pick one or more of the following.

- Describe  the  most  emotionally  significant  moment  in  the game for your protagonist. If it scared them, gain a horror. If you think there will be effects from it that continue into the future, describe them and gain a foreboding. If you overcame an obstacle, either through your own skill or with help from another protagonist or an NPC, describe how you did it and gain a humanity.
- Make any number of other revelations.
