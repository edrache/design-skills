## Character

For character creation, as a group you go through all of these questions and each answer them. You’ll each write down your own answers to each of the questions that tells you to gain a tag on your character sheet, which you can find on page 252. Tags are either positive (in which case they help you to Investigate or Face Peril), negative (in which case they hinder you, but grant you a humanity each time they’re invoked), or wild (which could go either way).

By the end of character creation you’ll have five positive tags, one wild tag, and your choice of one, two, or three negative tags. You’ll also have at least one bond.

All of the questions that give you tags have examples you can be inspired by or pick from.

## Bonds

Bonds represent connections with other protagonists based on shared experience. A bond is a wild tag that both of your characters have and which you can only invoke if you’re cooperating on a roll (see Assisting Someone on page 75).
Each player can create a single bond at any point during character creation. If someone says something about their character that inspires you, offer to have your characters be connected with a bond. Describe how you think your characters are connected, come up with a short descriptive phrase to use as a tag, and if they accept, both of you write down that bond.
Because you’ll create a bond and every other player will also create one, you will on average start with two bonds in a given game. You might end up with more, but the fewest you can have is one. You could even end up with multiple bonds with the same character!

## Flowchart

The character creation questions follow the flowchart to the right. Outlined numbers represent questions that don't give you a tag themselves, but instead change which question you answer next. Solid numbers represent questions that give you tags.

Everyone starts with question 1, which gives you a positive tag and leads to question 2.

Question 2 doesn't give you a tag. Instead, it prompts you to  pick  which you care the most about: knowledge, wealth, or power. If you pick knowledge, you'll answer questions 3 and 6. Money will give you 4 and 7, while power will give you 5 and 8.  Whichever  pair  of  questions  you  answer,  you'll  get  another positive tag and another negative tag.

Question 9 splits the flowchart again. If you want to gain another negative tag, then you'll answer question 10, and if not, you'll skip it.

Everyone answers question 11 and gainst a positive tag. Question 12 is the final place where the flowchart splits. If you want to gain a final negative tag, you answer question 13, and if not, you skip it.

Everyone  answers  question  14  and  gains  a  positive  tag. Same with question 15.

Finally,  everyone  answers  question  16  and  gains  a  wild tag.

At the end, you'll have five positive tags, one, two, or three negative tags, and one wild tag.
