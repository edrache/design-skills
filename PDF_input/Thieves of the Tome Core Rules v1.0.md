## Tһе Ваѕісѕ

hieves of the Tome is a roleplaying game intended for one or two short sessions. It is about incompetent thieves stealing limitless

magic  potential  from  highly  competent mages and not knowing what to do with it. It is an acid-punk love letter to books. This game provides a way to dive into your favorite books, steal magic from evil people, and  come  out  the  other  side  laughing triumphantly.

## All you need to play is:

- 3-4 hours
- 2-6 players
- Writing implements and paper
- Several 6-sided dice
- One book per player, each with clear chapters or sections
- (optionally) Printed character sheets, free PDF at firstpancake.studio/thieves

## Who am I playing as?

The  players  embody  thieves  who  have stolen T omes from the Library of Na'zar. These T omes will be books that other players have brought to the table. While thieves can read and understand the names of the spells written in the T omes, and can even attempt to  cast  them,  what happens when they unleash this magic will be a mystery until the moment it is cast. Magic is a fickle thing.

Examples will appear on pieces of parchment like this!

## Is there a GM?

Yes, one player will be the Game Master (GM). They will embody Na'Zar, the first scribe of the T omes and original writer of destiny. They will describe the world, portray characters around the players, and act as the ultimate adjudicator of the game using their chosen book. The number one rule to GMing TotT: when in doubt, read a sentence from your T ome.

## What happens during a session?

The thieves will try to escape with their stolen Tomes,  fleeing  from  sorcerous despots called the White Guard. The meat of this game occurs when the thieves are forced to use  spells that they  don't understand in order to solve problems. Most of the time, this will result in some truly catastrophic mayhem.

- If you have ever desired to be a witch, mage, or sorcerer, then this game is for you. If you have a love of books, then this game is for you.  If  you  love  to  watch  a  disaster  of incompetence  unfold  and  laugh  at  the consequences, then this game is definitely for you. If you want to roar through prismatic multidimensional  chaos  on  a  chariot  of molten glass, then this game is certainly for you.

## Na'zar the Great Scrivener

Before the first word was put to parchment, before the world was  shattered  into 8,888,888,888 prismatic shards, there was no one but Na'zar. Na'zar sat lonesome upon a throne of opal, staring into The Alabaster White. They stared until their vision blurred, until blankness folded over emptiness, and, in layering the two, swimming colors formed from  the  void.  Na'zar's  eyes  filled  with images of dripping primordial dreams, and their head swelled with fantasia.

The only recourse to empty their brain was to write, to pour crawling chaos onto the page.

And so, the Great Scrivener put quill to paper and wrenched forth a story: the First Tome. It wasn't a good story; in fact, you or I might call it derivative or dull, but it was a  T ome,  a  container  of  an  entire  world created by Na'zar. They were proud of it, and it made them feel much better to have emptied  their  mind  of  such  unfettered possibility .

This worked for a while, and Na'zar enjoyed relative peace, but their brain filled with swarming visions over and over again. Each time they put quill to parchment, pen to notepad, fingers to keyboard, and wrote; encapsulating little portions of thoughts made real.

They wrote tragedies, romances, plays, field guides,  brochures,  and  crafts  books,  all containing their own worlds. As The Great Scrivener cemented their namesake, The Alabaster White receded, and the pressure on Na'zar's mind grew less. They wrote until 8,888,888,888 T omes were written. As the ink dried on the last page, Na'zar's fantasia escaped him for the final time.

The pressure was gone. They had scribed the  T omes  and  had  brought  forth  all Tomealities. The Alabaster White tormented them with visions no longer. Na'zar now sleeps  in  the  center  of  all  T omealities, surrounded by the True T omes.

## Tomes and Tomealities

I mentioned before that each  Tome contained a world. This was not metaphoric, figurative,  or  symbolic.  Every  piece  of written work literally contains an entire world. The Lord of The Rings truly exists somewhere in the T omealities, and, though it  was  written  by  J.R.R.  T olkien  in  our Tomeality, he was channeling a True T ome that Na'zar wrote in the aeons before us.

There exists a world, a T omeality, where hobbits, dragons, and elves are as real as us. Just so, your grandparent's Guide to Making Fabulous Handbags contains its own world of twisting sentient yarn balls, felted fields, and folding fabric rivers.

There is a difference between a T ome and every other piece of written work. While all written work contains a T omeality, only a Tome can be used to channel aspects of that Tomeality.  A  T ome  is  typically,  but  not always,  the  888th  printed  version  of  a written work. Through the use of Spellcraft, a T ome can be used to bring elements of its Tomeality to life.

This book is full of acid-punk marginalia. Many have stat blocks and can be used in your games. See page 57 for more on Antagonists.

## Libraries of Na'zar

Every T omeality has a place where T omes are hoarded and kept under guard: a Library of Na'zar. The T omes are incredibly powerful items and are thus coveted in every world. Like flies to carrion, the rich and powerful are pulled to the T omes and the myriad possibilities they can conjure. The Libraries of Na'zar are secure places where those who hold power inevitably gather all the T omes within a T omeality .

I don't know why it always happens this way, but consider that powerful entities never want there to be other powerful entities. Sometimes these powerful entities are open about their rule, flaunting the powers of the Tomes for all to see. Other times, those in charge rule in secret, using their Spellcraft to subtly shift the world to their liking.

Every world inevitably gets disgruntled with those  in  power.  Enough  nose  to  the grindstone and something is bound to break. Try though they might, the White Guard is never able to suppress all information about the T omes of a world. Eventually, a group of (dubiously) competent people learn about them and decide, "hell, I ought to take some of this power for myself."

These emboldened sorts are our thieves, a group of criminals that conclude it is their lot to break into the Libraries of Na'zar and take some of the magical goodness within for themselves. The Players in this game will portray the thieves, and we will follow their journey to escape from the White Guard with their newfound power, and hopefully lives, intact.

Regardless of how they operate, we'll call this group the White Guard. They're the antagonists in all T omealites, the ultimate users and abusers of power. They're the bad guys. The Libraries and the White Guard are the reason you don't see everyday people running  around  with  T omes  channeling chaos (they're also the reason the 888th print edition of a book sells really well on eBay).

Unfortunately, this tends to make things rather dull for everyone else. Fortunately for the collective boredom of the rest of the world, there are usually good guys too.

## Setup (30 minutes - 1 hour)

1. The Spell Library Phase Each player goes through the book they brought and writes spells for the first six chapters. They write these effects on a spell sheet, keeping them secret. The players then hand their spell sheets to the GM and place their  T ome  in  the  center  of  the  table. (Chapter 2)
2. Na'zar's Introduction The GM introduces the T omeality and the escape condition the thieves must complete to end the game. (Chapter 2 &amp; Chapter 5)
3. Character Creation Phase The players create their thieves by stealing a Tome other than the one they brought from the  center  of  the  table,  and  using  its contents to describe their thief. The players then go around in a circle and narrate how they stole the T omes. (Chapter 3)

## The Escape (2 - 3 hours)

1. The Beginning The story and game begin right after the thieves snatch the T omes off the shelves, following the heist  in  the  Character  Creation  Phase. (Chapter 4)
2. The Middle The players describe how their thieves pursue their destinies and the T omeality's escape condition. The GM presents challenges and obstacles. The players respond in kind by casting spells or acting. (Chapter 4 for Players &amp; Chapter 5 for GM)
3. The End Play continues until all thieves die or they achieve their destinies and the escape condition set by the GM. (Chapter 4 for Players &amp; Chapter 5 for GM)

## Anatomy of a Spell

Each  chapter  or  section  of  a  book  is considered a single spell. At the start of the game, each player will write the effects of spells for the book they brought. They write these in secret, so that only they know what the spells in that book do. Each spell is made of three parts:

Name The  name  of  the  spell  is  the chapter title (or, if it does not have one, the first sentence in that section).

CJ has brought Moby Dick to the table. She is a player, so she starts writing a spell for the first chapter. The first chapter is called 'Loomings', so this is also the name of the first spell in the book.

Choice A statement that prompts the caster to choose something. T ypically the choice determines targets, but a choice can also be more abstract than that. This is written as a statement that must start with the word 'Choose…'

'Loomings'. This means something that is about to happen. CJ has an idea of what the spell could do. For the choice she writes a sentence starting with 'Choose.' She writes 'Choose a person you can see and a type of natural disaster'

Effect A description of what the spell does. This should be based on the name of the spell.

For the effect of 'Loomings', CJ bases it on the idea of impending disaster and the themes of Moby Dick. She writes, 'The chosen person becomes paranoid and certain that the chosen natural disaster will happen within one hour. They begin preparing frantically.'

The spell writer then puts that book in the center of the table and hands the written spells to Na'zar. The writer of spells must keep them secret. Afterward, another player will steal that book from the center of the table and use it as their T ome for the rest of the game.

CJ has finished writing each of the spells for Moby Dick. She hands the written spells to Tim, the Game Master, and places her book in the middle of the table. The other player, Maddie, places her book, Frankenstein, in the middle of the table. CJ steals Frankenstein and Maddie steals Moby Dick. CJ will use Frankenstein to create her thief and to cast spells from for the rest of the game. Since Maddie wrote the spells for Frankenstein, CJ will only know the names of spells and can only make educated guesses at what they do.

## Casting a Spell

Only the wielder of the T ome may cast spells with it, but they will not know what those spells do. They are a thief; they do not know the intricacies of arcane lore. When the player that received the T ome wishes to cast a spell, this is what happens:

1. The thief speaks the name of the spell.
2. The GM reads the choice statement to the players.
3. The thief chooses accordingly.
4. The GM reads the effect to the players.
5. The GM and the players decide what happens. The GM has the final call.
6. The spell is starred and cannot be cast again without incurring doom .

Through these casting steps, the final effect of a spell becomes a delightful combination of the spell writer's intent, the thief's poorly informed  usage  of  it,  and  the  GM's moderation. It can create truly catastrophic failures where a spell is used at the worst possible time, or create moments of mindmelding elation when the caster correctly infers how the writer intended the spell to be used.

Later in their game, CJ and Maddie's thieves have broken into the Grand Royal Museum to steal an important royal jewel, but the Red Robe is hot on their trail. CJ has an idea for a spell to cast from her book, and in the gruff voice of her character Mary, says,

'Ooh, I got it! I cast You will rejoice to hear that no disaster has accompanied the commencement of an enterprise which you have regarded with such evil forebodings.' (This spell title is the first sentence of Chapter 1 instead of a chapter title since Frankenstein has no chapter titles.)

The GM finds the spell on the spell sheet, then reads its choice to CJ, 'Great, so you have to choose something you're worried about.'

'Awesome. I thought it might do something like this. I'm worried about the Red Robe catching us,' she says.

The GM now reads the effect, 'Great so here's what's written in effect: the chosen worry will not come to pass during the current enterprise. I think that's straightforward enough; The Red Robe will not catch you while you are stealing the royal jewel. Is everyone happy with that interpretation?'

The thieves nod in agreement, happy to know the Red Robe will not interrupt this gig and can turn their attentions to the less arcane security before them. The GM stars the spell, CJ may not cast it again.

## Асtіng &amp; Rеасtіng

There are a lot of other things a thief may want to do aside from casting spells, though spells will certainly be the most powerful way of dealing with problems. When a thief wants to do something where uncertainty is involved, we roll a pool of dice to act . When the GM wants to do something to a thief, and the thief wants to stop that, we roll a pool of dice to react .

1. The player describes what their thief is doing to act or react .
2. The player starts with a dice pool of 1d6.
3. The player makes a case for adding bonus dice in the pool and the GM decides if the case is acceptable.
4. The GM subtracts hazard dice from the pool.
5. The player rolls and takes the highest result.

A  result  of  1-4  will  cause  failure  and setbacks  where  the  GM  dictates  what happens, and a result of 5-6 will allow the player to narrate the outcome of the re/ action.

Bonus  dice  are  added  to  the  pool  by adjectives , specialties , and starred destinies . Dice are subtracted from the pool by difficulty and starred dooms . The effect of spells can always add or remove dice due to their immense power.

## Dооm &amp; Dеѕtіnу

Thieves  will  have  several dooms and destinies on their character sheet taken from the T ome they stole. These are short phrases describing potential futures of the thief. They start unstarred and do not influence action or reaction rolls until they are starred .

Destiny is starred when the thief pursues goals and embraces the destiny written for them in their T ome. Doom is starred when the thief is harmed by failing a reaction .

## Tоmеаlіtіеѕ - А Мultіvеrѕе оf Ѕеttіngѕ

T omeality is a portmanteau of T ome and reality . It is literally a world contained within a book. The GM will use their own book to answer 4 core questions about the world the thieves dive into:

- What is the core genre of this world?
- Is magic considered overt or covert?
- What is the Library of Na'zar like?
- What is the White Guard like?

The GM's book will also be used to inspire obstacles , locations , actions , and the powers of the White Guard , the group of pursuing mages seeking to kill the thieves and return their stolen T omes to the Library. This game is structured so that the GM does not need to prepare ahead of time, but can quickly dive into the world of any book on their shelf as a potential setting.

While there is freedom to pursue any setting or tone, Thieves of the T ome always follows the same type of story: the escape of the thieves from the pursuing White Guard . The GM and players can freely explore a weird magical multiverse without feeling pressured  to  do  any  worldbuilding  or scenario preparation.

## Єffесt

Each player will then create an Effect for each spell  based on the name of the spell. Effects aren't rules intensive; just say what a spell does in simple language. Don't worry about how powerful a spell is, how much damage a spell might do, or other "gamey" things like that. This effect should be inspired by its name and do something related to that name.

## Tips for Fun Spell Effects

- Use word association with the chapter  title.  What  seems  obvious to you will seem inspired and unique to others!
- Skim the first page . Themes and  words  there may bring inspiration.
- Be weird. The T omealities are a big place, don't be afraid of the strange and unexplored.
- Be powerful. Don't worry about a spell being overpowered. They will almost certainly be used incorrectly.
- Be  funny. Don't  worry  about overserious useful effects. This game is meant to be a funny wizard romp, after all.

Exact wording is not important when taking phrases from books for your adjectives , destiny , and dooms . ## 5. Dеѕtіnу

The rest of your stolen T ome tells the stories of the many potential futures your thief may experience. It tells of their successes, their failures, and their greatest dreams. It tells of their destiny .

Destinies are short sentences, formatted like "When I &lt;condition&gt; , I will become &lt;descriptor&gt; ". For example: "When I am backed into a corner, I will become  vicious."  They represent the potential futures of your thief; conditions set that will change who your thief is when they are triggered.

Think of these like goals your thief should pursue during the session. They allow for your character's wants and desires to drive where the story goes.

## When I...

The first part of a destiny is the "When I \_\_\_\_" statement. This is a trigger that could hypothetically be achieved by your character during a game. It could be a goal or  a  simple  situational  trigger.  These represent important pivotal moments in your character's life. Each destiny starts unstarred , but once the "When I \_\_\_\_" portion of the destiny is triggered during gameplay, it is permanently starred . This is the goal you want to achieve.

## I Will Become ...

The second part of a destiny is the "I will become \_\_\_\_" statement. This is a descriptor like an adjective that can be used to gain bonus dice while acting or reacting  once  the  "When  I \_\_\_\_\_" portion of a destiny occurs during the game. "I will become \_\_\_\_\_" describes how your character changes after the triggering destiny is achieved.

## Writing Destinies

Flip to a random page at the beginning, middle, and end of the book. For each page write one destiny . T ake snippets of phrases and words from the page to fill in the blanks of  "When I \_\_\_\_, I will become \_\_\_\_". Always feel free to change tense or take phrases out of context.

Your thief's future is not just prosperity and happiness, there is also immense risk in their future. After all, stealing the T omes should not be taken lightly. Like destines, dooms describe what could happen to your thief. They start unstarred . Once starred , they can subtract hazard dice when acting or reacting. Unlike destinies, they do not have a triggering condition. They  will  be  starred  when  you  fail  a reaction .

An easy example is a "broken leg", but these could be  more  abstract  like  "sunken  eyes"  or "haunted mind".

## Саѕtіng Ѕреllѕ

The Thieves' T omes are their greatest asset. With them, they can cast deadly spells. Each spell can only be cast once . While there are no turns in this game, the GM has the right to say a spell cannot be cast at a given moment.

## 1. Speak the Name

To cast a spell a player must open their T ome to the first page of a chapter and read the spell name (chapter title or first sentence if no chapter title) aloud.

## 2. Hear the Choice

The  GM  finds  the  spell  on  that  T ome's spellsheet and silently reads the choice and effect . They read the choice aloud to the player. The player may  ask  for clarification and the GM is allowed to amend the choice based on how they understand the effect if it is unclear.

## 3. Make a Choice

The casting thief must make one or several choices depending on what the choice of the spell says. If it is not possible to make a choice in the moment, then the spell fails but can be tried again later. The original writer of the spell is not allowed to influence this choice.

## 4. Hear the Effect

The GM reads the effect of the spell as written to the players. If a spell reaches this point it is considered to have succeeded.

## 5. The Consequences

The players and GM discuss what exactly they think should happen based on the written effect and the thief's choice. The GM gets  the  final  say  in  this  narration,  but players are encouraged to share ideas on how they interpret the two. You can often come to a much more interesting outcome by listening to what everyone has to say.

## 6. Spell Consumed

After a spell's consequences have been narrated by the GM, the GM marks a star next to the spell on the appropriate spell sheet. That spell can only be recast at the cost of an additional doom (see next page).

## Асtіng

The other way players can interact with the world is through acting . When acting, a pool of dice is rolled to determine what happens when a thief tries to do something risky not involving their T omes. Only roll for acting when dramatically appropriate to resolve tension and uncertainty.

A  thief  doesn't  need  to  act  to  open  an unlocked door, but they will certainly need to act to open a magically sealed school locker.  The  GM  gets  the  final  say  over whether  a  described  narration  requires acting or not.

If a thief succeeds while acting, they gain narrative control over the uncertainty. If they fail while acting, the GM will introduce a complication and gain narrative control over the uncertainty.

## 1. The Thief Acts

The player describes what their thief is doing to act. What are they trying to accomplish, and how are they accomplishing it? They then start their dice pool with 1d6 .

## 2. Player Adds Bonus Dice

The player makes a case to the GM for bonus dice based on the sources on the right. Each source can only add 1d6 to the dice pool (i.e. a player can't make a case for 2d6 from the same  adjective).  Each ally can contribute a single bonus die if their thief is helping with the action and they have an appropriate source. The player makes a case for which (if any) of these bonus sources would  reasonably  improve  their  thief's chance of acting successfully. The GM then gets to decide which dice are added to the pool.

If a player can describe a reasonable way a bonus source contributes to an action in a sentence, the GM should allow that bonus die into the pool.

## 3. GM Subtracts Hazard Dice

The  GM  decides  how  many  dice  are subtracted based on the sources on the right. Dooms and lingering spell effects can only subtract 1d6, but the difficulty of the  action  can  subtract  as  many  as  is reasonable. The player's pool cannot go below 1d6. The GM must explain why they are removing each hazard die.

## 4. Roll The Dice Pool

The player rolls all dice together after adding bonus dice and subtracting hazard dice. The result of the roll is the highest number rolled.

## 5. The Outcome

Success (5-6): If the player rolls a 5-6, the thief succeeds in their action. The player gains the right to narrate the outcome of the  action,  and  describe  how  the  thief completes the action.

Failure (1-4): If the player rolls a 1-4, the thief fails in their action. The GM gains the right to narrate the outcome of the action, and how the thief fails to complete the action. They also introduce a complication .

To introduce a complication , the GM flips to a random page in their own book and places  their  finger  randomly  on  a  word there. They use this word to inspire the outcome  of  the  failure.  Thieves  should always  fail  in  a  way  that  creates  new problems to be solved or prevents them from attempting the same action in the same way again.

## Rеасtіng

When  the  GM  threatens  a  thief  with consequences,  any  thief  can react by rolling  a  pool  of  dice  to  prevent  those consequences.  The  GM  should  usually threaten consequences by saying "\_\_\_\_\_\_ will happen if you do nothing." However, any  time  a  player  wishes  to  contest something the GM narrates, they can roll a pool of dice to react regardless of whether the GM prompts for reactions.

If the player succeeds , they gain narrative control of the reaction and the thief does not suffer the consequences threatened by the GM. If the player fails , however, the threatened thief suffers the consequences and  the  reacting  thief stars  a  doom selected by the GM. If something is reacted to by a thief, it can not be reacted to again.

## 1. The Thief Reacts

If a thief wishes to react to something the GM has narrated, they say so and describe what their thief is doing to prevent what the GM  has  narrated.  A  thief  can  react  to consequences threatening themselves or any other thief.

## 2. Construct Dice Pool

Follow the same rules for constructing the dice pool as are used in acting . Add bonus dice , subtract hazard dice , then roll .

## 3. The Outcome

Success (5-6): If the player rolls a 5-6, the thief succeeds in their reaction. The player gains the right to narrate how their thief allowed themselves or others to avoid consequences

Failure (1-4): If the player rolls a 1-4, the thief fails to react. The threatened consequences proceed as the GM narrates. The GM also looks at the reacting thief's dooms for one appropriate to the situation. They then star this doom and narrate the harm that came to the thief for failing to react.

Destinies are  composed of "When I... &lt;condition&gt;, I  will  become... &lt;descriptor&gt; " statements. hieves of the Tome is a very reactive RPG from the perspective of the Game Master. When you run the game, you are responding to player goals and actions rather than designing an overall narrative. Spells are so powerful that they can override almost any preparation.

This chapter presents how to run a game using your chosen book. Your T ome will describe the Tomeality ,  the setting in which the thieves steal the T omes, as well as the Escape Condition , the condition thieves need to meet to escape with their lives. It will also describe The Palette , a tool that you can use to construct unique scenes from locations , obstacles , and actions . Lastly, the T ome will be used to construct Antagonists ,  agents  of  the White Guard who stand between the thieves and their plans.

The T omeality, Escape Condition, Palette, and Antagonists can be combined into a worksheet that can be completed while players  are  writing  spells  and  making characters.  This  worksheet  is  called  a Module . A Module is generally designed to be completed in one session. A blank GM sheet can be found at the end of this book.

## Yоur Tоmе

While the players do not need to have read their books to bring them to the table, it is suggested that you have read your book. Having familiarity with the setting will allow you  to be  more  creative  with  ideas generated from the book and allow for a clearer  vision  of  the  world.  The  only requirement, however, is that the book you use has named chapters or sections .

While the players are preparing for their heist  and  escape  by  writing  spells  and creating characters, you should prepare for the session. This allows Thieves of the T ome to be run without any prior planning. If you want to prepare ahead of time, however, you always can do most of the following steps before playing. Especially if this is your first time playing, filling out the Module beforehand can save a lot of stress.

## Wrіtіng tһе Tоmеаlіtу

The Tomeality is the setting of the world the thieves inhabit. This world is contained within the book you have brought to the table (as all worlds are contained within one of Na'Zar's books). Using your knowledge of the book, or the back blurb, you must answer the following questions about the Tomeality.

## What is the core genre of  the world?

Write the genre of the book you've brought to the table. Keep this to a couple of words at most. If the book you've brought is sci-fi, then the world is sci-fi.  If  the  book  you brought is a guide to knitting handbags, then maybe the world is fabricpunk fantasy. This should be a basic touchstone for you and the players to reference when in doubt about the tone of the world.

## Is magic overt or covert?

Using the genre, decide if the magic of the Tomes in this world is overt or covert. Overt magic is something that everyone is aware of. Individuals may even legally be able to acquire T omes. Covert magic is something that the White Guard have kept hidden from society. Generally, fantasy genres will have overt magic while others like cyberpunk will have covert magic.

## What is the White Guard like?

Describe the White Guard based on any descriptions  of  antagonists  from  your knowledge  of  the  book.  What  class  of society are they from? Are they secretive string pullers or a public oligarchy? Do they have followers? What goals are they working toward? Ultimately try to boil this down to a sentence or two.

The  royal  family  of  England seeking to live forever.

A megacorp fixated on profit margins and  commodifying magic.

A secretive sect aiming to control world affairs.

An artisan guild that only accepts the best of the best.

## What is the Library of Na'Zar like?

Describe the Library of Na'Zar using any locations mentioned in the book or pulled from the genre it evokes. What type of building is it in? What are its basic defenses that might make a heist difficult? What is unique about it that ties into or subverts the genre? Again boil this down to a sentence or two.

A hollowed-out mushroom rife with rabid worshipers.

## Using the Palette

When the players set goals in the game you can respond by using the palette . ## The White Guard

The White Guard are mages guarding the secrets of the T omes. They exist in every Tomeality, and horde the books of those realities. Mages in the White Guard have the following critical qualities:

## Ѕtаrtіng Tһе Gаmе

The thieves always start in the Library of Na'Zar, moments after snatching their books from the shelves. The last step of character creation is describing the heist that just happened.  The  GM  will  start  this  by describing the library, making sure to note any obvious defenses so the players know what their thieves had to overcome to get in. Feel free to let players add to the world here, and invent new defenses that they triumphed over.

Once the characters are complete, you can dive right into the game. As a suggestion, have the players face one obstacle on their way out of the library. If they draw attention to themselves in any way, make them draw the notice of an antagonist .

## Gоаl Ѕеttіng

Once the players have escaped the library, remind them of their escape condition and that they must each star a destiny . Have the players decide which goal they would like to pursue first. Once players have a goal, they can act or cast spells to find the location where the goal can be achieved.

## Ѕсеnеѕ

Scenes can be created using the palette . Once at a location , the thieves will have to overcome some obstacle to get to their goal. Usually, one or more antagonists will interfere. Actions can always be added to spice up uninteresting NPCs or antagonists. Here are some example scene templates:

The thieves arrive at location and find obstacle in their way. Just when they start to enact their plan, antagonist shows up.

The thieves arrive at location and  find  an antagonist is already there, and has set up obstacle to  protect the goal from the thieves.

The thieves arrive at location and find  their  goal  is  on  the move.  They  must  chase  it. Antagonist chases them next. Obstacle makes  the  chase harder for everyone.

## Tһrеаtѕ &amp; Tеmро

As the GM, you do not roll in Thieves of the Tome. Players always do all the rolling. T o challenge the thieves, you must threaten them. "The robe is swinging a wickedly barbed blade at you. if you do nothing, I will star a doom ." This is a clear threat that indicates to the player something bad will happen and they need to react . Threats may do any of the following and more:

- Star a thief's doom
- Allow an enemy to cast a spell
- Destroy items or equipment
- Destroy or make a goal harder

According to the tempo of the scene, you should narrate a new threat every time the thieves have collectively taken action. For instance in 2:1 tempo, if one thief has cast a spell and another has taken an action, you should then narrate one new threat.

## Оnlіnе Рlау

Sometimes printed works are replicated on the crawling bitbanged chaos that connects us modern people instead of on paper. Even represented  as  electric  breath  on  cold silicon, the words of Na'Zar have power. Because Thieves of the T ome uses exchange of physical books, it has some unique needs to support online play. This section covers all you need to play online.

## Book Sources

There are many sources for online books that you can share with your friends. Here is a list of legal, free, and easy to use ones.

## · Project Gutenburg (www.gutenberg.org)

Full books in online, epub, ebook, pdf, or plaintext format. No account is needed to use. Most public domain books can be found here. It is a fun website to trawl for odd texts.

## · Internet Archive

## (www.archive.org)

Free or borrowable books in online, epub, ebook, pdf, or plaintext formats. An account is needed for borrowing items. This can allow more modern books to be found.

## · Hoopla

## (www.hoopladigital.org)

Use your public library card to access eBooks  and  magazines.  This  will definitely  have  the  most  modern options if looking for particular books.

## Online Book Exchange

If you wish to play purely online with minimal financial  investment  or  changes  to  the game, have the players select their books from one of the above sources that everyone can  access.  Next,  have  players  fill  out spellsheets as usual. When it comes time to exchange the books, players can send book links to whatever online tool they prefer for voice and text chat, and a copy of the spellsheet PDF to the GM. Players can then claim books for character creation and spellcasting from the list of links.

## Mail Book Exchange

A perhaps more interesting way to play this game and profess your love of books at the same time is to use it as a book exchange. Randomly assign each player another player as their receiver . Each player selects a book that they wish to give to their receiver and think the receiver would enjoy. The player writes a spellsheet for this book like normal.

Then, they send a PDF of the spellsheet to the GM, and mail the book to their receiver. Once everyone has received their books, the game can be played from the T omeality Introduction step using the books that were just mailed out. Decide upfront with the players whether they will keep the books or mail them back.

## Other Tips

All Thieves of the T ome sheets are available as form fillable PDFs at firstpancake.studio/ thieves. These can be filled out and sent to other players using any file exchange or messaging application. Since online play tends to slow things down, it is often better to have players write spells and send you the spell sheets in advance of the game. In addition, I recommend preparing your GM sheet ahead of the game for online play to ensure it goes as smoothly as possible.

## Маgісаl Rеtіrеmеnt

At the end of the game the thieves have hopefully escaped with their books, reaching their  destiny  and  completing  the  heist. Spend one quick scene for each player at the end of the game showing their magical retirement. Think of this like the montage at  the  end  of  the  heist  movie  with  the thieves  enjoying  their  cocktails  on  the beach, but instead of money or jewels, the thieves have powerful spellbooks to build their ideal future.

Assume the players:

- Know what all their spells do.
- Can recast spells.
- Never expend spells.
