## FOREWORD

I used to joke that I grew up in the woods from Th e Blair Witch Project .

It is my hope that both new and returning Call of Cthulhu players discover an aspect of horror that they may not have encountered before, and I especially hope they find inspiration with which to weave their own tales of Mythos horror at the gaming table.

Alone Against the Static is my attempt to view the quiet wild through the lens of the Cthulhu Mythos. I wanted to give players a strong sense of place and isolation. Setting the scenario in the 1990s allowed me to showcase how Call of Cthulhu can flourish in all settings-the modern age opens new avenues of horror not available in the 1920s, such as creepy video tapes of unknown origin.

-B. W. Holland, Melbourne, 2022

Our family home was locked in by state forest for two kilometers in every direction. Growing up in a place where the darkness was thick, and the sounds beyond the walls of our house were often unidentifiable, I came to appreciate a level of horror that manifests in the wild places of the world that-to me-feels absent in cities populated by the human throng. I learned to savor the feeling of protection while inside a home in such locations, however false that safety actually was. It's a sensation of the walls of wood and mudbrick somehow keeping the cold and the darkness at bay, even if the sound of something raking gently across your bedroom window was… commonplace.


## INTRODUCTION

Alone  Against  the  Static is  a Call  of  Cthulhu scenario designed to be played by a single person, where you are both the Keeper and player. You take on the role of either Alex or Charlie-a young couple who are vacationing at a secluded forest  cabin  in  South  Dakota's  Black  Hills. Th e  collapse of their relationship, and a creeping horror growing in the woods around them, are the central focus of the adventure. Chaosium recommends this book for mature players.

You can attempt this adventure as many times as you like. Each playthrough allows you to select different choices or even  a  different  character.  With  every  new  path  you  take, you'll discover more secrets hidden away among the pines. Make good use of the tools at your disposal if you hope to survive this very unfortunate vacation.

Th e  adventure  is  set  during  the  1990s;  unfortunately for you, it takes place far out of signal range. Don't rely on modern conveniences  to  protect  you  from  danger.  Deadly forces to whom a few decades mean nothing are lurking in the shadowy woods around you.

## GETTING STARTED

You'll  need  a  pencil,  scratch  paper,  dice,  and  the Call  of Cthulhu: Keeper Rulebook or the Call of Cthulhu Starter Set .

Don't read this book through from beginning to end! Each entry is numbered, and at its end you'll find instructions for where to go and what to read next. Usually, you will be asked to choose an action from between two or more options, and then turn to a corresponding entry based on your decision. Sometimes, you'll see additional instructions, like attempting a  skill  roll.  When  this  happens,  follow  the  instructions  as written.

Before  you  begin,  you  must  first  select  either  Alex  or Charlie. You can find their investigator sheets on pages 102 and 104. Your story will be different depending on which character  you  choose.  While  both  Alex  and  Charlie  may encounter  the  same  obstacles,  they  each  have  a  range  of different skills and characteristics, making every playthrough unique. Th e  choices  you  make  as  you  play  are  not  always concerned  with  what  your  character  is  doing  but  may  be to  decide  how  your  character  is  feeling,  which  can  have  a bearing on how things progress.

## THE LOG SHEET

You should always start any playthrough with a blank log sheet. If you restart your adventure, make sure to erase all the checked boxes.

Alone Against the Static uses a log sheet (page 98) to help record  the  choices  you've  made  during  your  playthrough. As  you  progress  through  the  adventure,  some  entries instruct you to check/tick a specific box on the log sheet. Some entries provide you with extra options depending on which boxes on the log sheet are already checked/ticked. Some entries may even block you from certain choices and force  you  to  turn  to  a  particular  entry,  all  based  on  what prerequisites you've fulfilled.

## MAKING A ROLL

Some Call  of  Cthulhu mechanics  have  been  simplified to  streamline gameplay. In combat for example, only the result of your combat rolls factor into deciding an outcome. Other mechanics, like chases, bouts of madness, and more, have been similarly simplified. Any of these modifications to  standard  mechanics,  as  well  as  any  additional  factors like bonus or penalty dice, are clearly stated alongside the regular instructions in each entry.

At  various  points  throughout  this  adventure,  you  may be  asked  to  make  skill  or  characteristic  rolls. Th ese  rolls are  made  as  per  normal  for Call of  Cthulhu ,  using  either Charlie's or Alex's investigator sheet.

## PUSHING ROLLS &amp; SPENDING LUCK

If you don't want to push a roll, you can also use Luck as per the optional Luck rules in the Call of Cthulhu: Keeper Rulebook . Th is rule allows you to spend your Luck points to alter the result of skill or characteristic rolls. Y ou may alter a roll on a 1-for-1 basis. For example, to adjust a roll of 49 to make the result equal to your skill of 45, you would spend 4 points of Luck, and deduct that amount from your current Luck total. You cannot spend Luck points to alter Damage, Luck, or Sanity rolls, or rolls to determine the amount of Sanity points lost. Additionally, you cannot spend Luck on a pushed roll. You may use Luck spending in this manner whenever you desire during the adventure-but be aware that your Luck can run out!

If you fail a roll, you may be able to either spend Luck or Push the Roll to succeed-the entry text advises you if a pushed roll is possible. If a roll is eligible to be pushed, then when you are asked to make it, you will also be invited to reattempt a failure at the risk of additional penalties.

## DYING &amp; GOING INSANE

Over the course of the scenario, some choices can result in a loss of either Hit Points or Sanity . Track these values carefully and remember the following:

- If you ever reach zero hit points, you fall unconscious, become defenseless, and will die. Mark down the entry number you're in, then go to 324 .
- If you are ever reduced to zero Sanity , you go permanently insane. Mark down the passage you're in, then go to 334 .
- If you ever lose half or more of your health in a single instance of damage, you receive a major wound. Mark down the entry number you're in, then go to 325 .
- If you ever lose more than one fifth of your total Sanity in a single day, you go indefinitely insane. Mark down the entry you're in, then go to 328. Charlie 's Sanity begins at 60, which means one-fifth of their total Sanity is 12. Alex 's Sanity begins at 50, which means one-fifth of their total Sanity is 10. These values may change throughout the adventure due to lesser Sanity losses.
- If you ever lose more than 5 Sanity points in a single instance, you trigger a bout of madness. Mark down the entry you're in, then go to 329 .

It  is  possible  to  trigger  several  of  the  above  conditions simultaneously.  If  this  occurs,  resolve  the  conflicts  in  the following order:

- Prioritize resolving damage before Sanity.
- Prioritize reaching zero Sanity first, which basically cancels out indefinite insanity and/or a bout of madness (i.e., go to 334 not 328 or 329).
- Prioritize reaching zero hit points over resolving a major wound (i.e., go to 324 not 325 ).

Waiting in the isolated and lonely wilds of the world, the Child of  the  Forest  lives  in  the  forgotten  winds,  the  unturned  earth, and ancient trees, where all other life fears to tread. It aches for purpose  and  form  and  desires  a  violent  unending  to  its  own accursed solitude.

Th e  being  known  as  Ush-Tik-A-or  the  Child  of  the Forest-is  an  ancient entity said to inhabit the wild places of the world. Born from a mixture of dark rites cast centuries ago over the remnants of a fallen star, Ush-Tik-A is a shapeless, formless hive mind that poisons the regions it inhabits, rendering it free from animal and human life.

Ush-Tik-A  is  a  lonely  entity.  It  desires  the  ability  to take physical form, but even with a willing host, this never lasts  long,  as  Ush-Tik-A's  unfamiliarity  with  solid  shapes inevitably destroy the host in a warping and violent calamity of broken bones and mutated flesh.

Its  connection  to  the  ley  lines  of  the  earth  extends  its consciousness  through  time,  allowing  it  to  view  its  own future,  past,  and  present  at  once.  While  it  can  only  take physical form by inhabiting the body of a human host, it can manipulate natural minerals to communicate.

## Cult

Members  of  the  cult  are  generally  driven  by  a  desire to  assist  the  being  in  undoing  its  loneliness,  while  more narcissistic followers are motivated by the gift of foresight. Above  all  else,  those  who  show  promise  in  an  ability  to procure other sacrifices are generally spared being devoured by Ush-Tik-A in favor of them bringing new sacrifices into the fold.

Th ere  are  few  followers  of  Ush-Tik-A.  Usually,  disciples are  those  looking  to  connect  with  spirits  of  the  wild. Occasionally, the Child of the Forest answers their search, wearing  a  guise  to  gain  the  trust  of  potential  followers, before then communicating with them about its solitude to garner sympathy.

## Encounters

It  is  possible  to  reside  in  Ush-Tik-A's  domain for some time before succumbing to the disease, as the entity always prioritizes its desire to consume souls before its desire to kill.

Ush-Tik-A  poisons  the  wild  in  which  it  resides,  slowly killing all insect and animal life with a disease that causes horrid weeping welts, followed (most of the time) by violent aneurysms as the brain contorts and bleeds to death.

## DYING &amp; GOING INSANE

Over the course of the scenario, some choices can result in a loss of either Hit Points or Sanity . Track these values carefully and remember the following:

- If you ever reach zero hit points, you fall unconscious, become defenseless, and will die. Mark down the passage you're in, then C go to 324 .
- If you are ever reduced to zero Sanity , you go permanently insane. Mark down the passage you're in, then go to 334 .
- If you ever lose half or more of your health in a single instance of damage, you receive a major wound. Mark down the passage C you're in, then go to 325 .
- If you ever lose more than one fifth of your total Sanity in a single day, you go indefinitely insane. Mark down the passage C you're in, then go to 328 . Charlie T Sanity begins at 60, which means one-fifth of their total Sanity is 12. Alex 's Sanity begins at 50, which means one-fifth of their total Sanity is 10. These values may change throughout the adventure due to lesser Sanity losses.
- If you ever lose more than 5 Sanity points in a single instance, you trigger a bout of madness. Mark down the passage you're in, C then go to 329 .

It is possible to trigger several of the conditions above all at once. If this occurs, resolve the conflicts in the following order:

- Prioritize resolving damage before Sanity.
- Prioritize reaching zero Sanity first, which basically cancels out indefinite insanity and/or a bout of madness (i.e., go to 334 not 328 or 329 ).
- Prioritize reaching zero hit points over resolving a major wound (i.e., go to 324 not 325 ).
